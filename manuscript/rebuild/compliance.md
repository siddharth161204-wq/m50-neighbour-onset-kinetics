**The procedures and the supplement.** The guide asks for "detailed procedures necessary to replicate the study, as well as additional results such as sensitivity analyses" in an appendix rather than in supplementary material, which it keeps for videos, animations, interactive files and the like. Every procedure that sets a reported number is now described in the main text, its tables or its appendices at the level needed to repeat it: the model's equations, control volumes and approximations (Sections 2.2 to 2.5, Appendices A and B), the fit of each trigger (Section 2.1), how the trigger's rise times are derived from the published records and how the calorimetry is emulated (two rows of Table 2, added in Brief 32 at no cost in words, since tables are not counted), the criteria, the definitions of a threshold and a need and their brackets' widths (Section 2.4), the bounds' settings, with the ray tracing named (Table 4), the jump rule and how the history was read (Fig. 5's caption), and the checks and the held-out test (Section 2.7). These details remain only in the supplement, measured as they stand by `word_count.words()`: the ray-tracing grid (Section S6, 235 words), the two readings of the history and their uncertainty (S7, 198) and the jump rule's reading of the record (S7, 155), the order in which the searches for the needs tried their energies (S9, 67) and the local threshold searches' stopping width (S10, 40), the held-out fits' penalised likelihood and permutation test (S11, 56), the grid of the three-dimensional searches (S17, 232), and the rest of the emulated calorimetry's check (S2, about 316 words before Table 2's row). The sensitivity analyses' key values are in the main text (Section 2.7, Fig. 4b, Section 4.4), and their full tables in the supplement (Tables S4, S12 and S13). Moving those details into an Appendix C as they stand would add about 1,300 words and take the main text and appendices to about 9,300, beyond the 8,000 that one main text for both journals must keep. Whether to make an appendix for this journal alone, or to submit with the supplement as it is, is the author's decision.

## Not met, and open

1. **The Fire Safety Journal's page preference.** Its guide prefers original research papers of no more than 20 double-spaced pages including tables and illustrations; the rebuilt manuscript is 42. Reaching 20 would mean cutting about two-fifths of the paper, for example three of the seven figures, the two appendices and about a third of the main text, and so dropping one of the six results sections. The guide says "preferably", and the manuscript meets the other two journals' limits as it stands; if the Fire Safety Journal is chosen, the main chat decides whether to cut or to submit at this length.
2. **The Journal of Energy Storage and the supplement.** That journal asks for "detailed procedures necessary to replicate the study, as well as additional results such as sensitivity analyses" in an appendix, and keeps the supplement for media. If it is chosen, Supplementary Sections S5 (the checks), S6 (the bound inputs), S7 (the digitisation), S9 (the needs by shape) and S10 (the local threshold against the reduced models) would move into an Appendix C as they are, adding four tables; the figures and tables together would then be 14, above that journal's ideal of 8 to 10, and the main text and appendices together would pass its 6,000 words.
3. **The Journal of Energy Storage's ideal length.** Its guide says "ideally" a maximum of 6,000 words; the fixes after the second and third independent reads (the needs after the weak trigger, the brackets' ranges, the bounds' conditions, the definitions of the need and the jump, the origins of some two dozen numbers) brought the main text to 6,969, and Brief 30's introduction, lead sentences and shares to 7,373. The Journal of Power Sources' 8,000 is met. If the Journal of Energy Storage is chosen, the numerical details of Section 2.2 and the bounds' derivations of Section 2.6 could move into the appendices, which its count leaves out.
4. **The author's own entries.** The postal address of the affiliation (an independent researcher's own address, with the country), where a journal's system asks for it; the generative AI statement under its heading, and the CRediT line that must agree with it; and the competing-interest form in the submission system.
