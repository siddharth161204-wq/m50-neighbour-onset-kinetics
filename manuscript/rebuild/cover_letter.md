# Cover letters (drafts, not sent; 24 November 2026)

One letter per journal; they differ only in the editor addressed, the sentence on the journal's scope and, for the Journal of Power Sources, whose guide asks that the letter carry no funding information or author declarations, the end of the sentence about the author. Word files of each are in `letters/`.

## Journal of Power Sources (Ilias Belharouak, Editor-in-Chief; 480 words)

Dear Dr Belharouak,

I submit the manuscript "Thermal runaway propagation between two lithium-ion cells: resolved and lumped neighbour models against sixteen tests" for consideration as a research article in the Journal of Power Sources.

Models of thermal runaway propagation in modules must choose how much of a failing cell's vented heat reaches its neighbour, which published tests do not measure, and must represent each cell as one temperature or as a resolved temperature field. The manuscript asks how much heat must reach a neighbour for it to follow, and whether published tests can say, for two touching LG M50 cells modelled with kinetics fitted to calorimetry of NCM811 and graphite, against sixteen published two-cell tests.

Each failing cell's energy is fitted to its own measured peak temperature, 31.6 to 54.3 kJ in fourteen tests (strong triggers), mostly above the 33.2 kJ that calorimetry measured at most for this cell, and 9.0 and 16.6 kJ in two (weak), and the neighbour is solved both as one temperature and as a three-dimensional field; in each, the heat it needs through its side facing the failing cell to run away at the test's measured time is searched, and the bounds that published inputs set on each path that could deliver it are worked out.

At either energy, conduction and radiation run no resolved neighbour away, so heat must also arrive through its side facing the failing cell. With heat delivered over 21.4 s, the mean of the two rise times read from the two published records, the eight tests that propagated after strong triggers need 4.45 to 7.54 kJ if the neighbour is resolved in three dimensions and 0.26 to 5.96 kJ if it is lumped. To run away at all, the resolved neighbour needs at least 1.36 times the lumped neighbour's energy after the four strong triggers searched in three dimensions and at most 0.91 times after both weak ones, so in these tests a heat share calibrated with one representation needs a correction that depends on the trigger before it can serve the other. Published inputs rule out no path delivering 27.9 kJ to the facing side within 21.4 s.

For readers who model propagation in packs, the paper gives the heat a neighbour needs under each of the two common representations of a cell, set against sixteen tests, the factor between them and its direction, and the measurement, a heat flux on the neighbour's facing side, that could test them.

It falls within the journal's priority area of safety, as a computational study set against published experiments.

The code, inputs and run records are archived openly, with the study's 109 predictions, each committed before its runs.

I am a mechanical engineer (B.Tech 2026, COEP Technological University, Pune), working independently. The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere.

Yours sincerely,

Siddharth Satte
Independent researcher, Pune, India
