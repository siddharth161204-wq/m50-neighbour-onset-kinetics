# Cover letters (drafts, not sent; 20 November 2026)

One letter per journal; they differ only in the editor addressed, the sentence on the journal's scope and, for the Journal of Power Sources, whose guide asks that the letter carry no funding information or author declarations, the end of the sentence about the author. Word files of each are in `letters/`.

## Journal of Power Sources (Ilias Belharouak, Editor-in-Chief; 490 words)

Dear Dr Belharouak,

I submit the manuscript "Modelling thermal runaway propagation between lithium-ion cells: a lumped neighbour needs less heat than a resolved one after strong triggers" for consideration as a research article in the Journal of Power Sources.

Propagation models of modules treat each cell as one temperature or a resolved field, and choose what share of the failing cell's vented heat reaches its neighbour. The manuscript asks how much delivered heat a neighbour needs under each treatment, for two touching LG M50 cells with decomposition kinetics fitted to another cell's calorimetry (NCM811 and graphite; the M50's negative electrode adds silicon), against sixteen published two-cell tests.

It finds that the choice matters, in a direction set by the trigger. With each test's trigger fitted to its measured peak, the resolved neighbour needs 1.5 and 1.7 times the heat of the lumped neighbour in the two tests with strong triggers (fitted body energies of 31.6 to 54.3 kJ) where both thresholds are bracketed, and 0.85 times in the one with a weak trigger. A share of ejected energy calibrated with one treatment therefore needs a trigger-dependent correction to serve the other. Reduced models point to the mechanisms: up to 120 s, conduction sets the resolved cell's hot spot, and the lumped cell's runaway agrees with an energy balance to an ignition temperature of 218.5 °C. Matching the two thresholds in one test would need every reaction to run as if over 17.5 K hotter, beyond the 6.1 K of the kinetics' published uncertainty, which omits the difference between their cell and the M50. At the central contact a two-dimensional reactive cross-section reproduced the outcome of all 151 archived three-dimensional runs at a fraction of their cost, making a resolved neighbour affordable in module models.

In the model, with the failing cell's energy held to calorimetry, conduction and radiation leave the neighbour at least 29 K short of runaway; the ejecta or other paths must supply the rest. Published inputs cannot bound any path through the neighbour's facing half below 27.9 kJ within 21.4 s, above what either treatment needs at the measured times, so the sixteen tests cannot decide between them. The one published record of a propagating neighbour fits neither cleanly, its final rise starting 37 K cooler than on the resolved model's far side; a heat flux measured on its facing side would decide, if the model's other inputs stand.

It falls within the journal's priority areas of safety and of multiscale simulation set against experiments: it couples a cell's decomposition kinetics to its heat transfer and compares the result with measured propagation tests.

The code, inputs and run records are archived openly, with the study's 98 predictions, each committed before its runs.

I am a mechanical engineer (B.Tech 2026, COEP Technological University, Pune), working independently. The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere.

Yours sincerely,

Siddharth Satte
Independent researcher, Pune, Maharashtra, India
siddharth161204@gmail.com
