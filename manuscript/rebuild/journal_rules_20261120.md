## 1. Scope

- **Journal of Power Sources.** "a premier global forum for publishing high-impact research and critical reviews that shape the future of electrochemical energy technologies", covering the value chain from materials to "full-system integration, performance validation, and real-world deployment". Priority areas include materials electrochemistry for "performance, safety, cost, efficiency, and reliability" and "multiscale simulations to accelerate discovery, enhance predictive modeling, and optimize system performance, substantiated by experimental validation". Sector-specific applications are in scope "only when they directly involve or advance the electrochemistry of materials and components". The journal "welcomes experimental, and experimentally validated theoretical and computational contributions".
- **Journal of Energy Storage.** "all aspects of energy storage, in particular systems integration, electric grid integration, modelling and analysis, novel energy storage technologies, sizing and management strategies". Listed topics include "Engineering, control, optimization, numerical and modelling aspects of energy storage systems" and "Testing, test procedures, evaluation, lessons learned, life cycle costs, life cycle assessment, and safety of energy storage systems". Energy storage must be "the central scientific contribution". It does not accept resubmissions of manuscripts it has peer reviewed and rejected, nor "Part I / Part II" papers.
- **Fire Safety Journal.** "An international journal devoted to research on Fire Safety Science and Engineering", the official journal of the International Association for Fire Safety Science, dealing "with all aspects of fire safety engineering"; topics for guidance include fire chemistry and physics, fire dynamics, assessment and quantification of fire risk, and fire safety design "(including consumer items, industrial plant, transportation, buildings)". Contributions are invited "particularly if they incorporate a quantitative approach".

## 4. Form of the manuscript

All three, the same text:
- Source files .docx or .tex ("A PDF is not an acceptable source file"); Word in a single column; no strikethrough or underline.
- Title page: a concise, informative title avoiding abbreviations and formulae; given and family names; affiliations where the work was done with "the full name and postal address of each affiliation, including the country name"; the corresponding author's e-mail address on the title page, which "will be published in the article". Institutional affiliations by their full standard title or standard abbreviation.
- Numbered sections and subsections (1.1, 1.1.1); the abstract not numbered; cross-references by section number.
- Equations as editable text, numbered consecutively; variables in italics; the solidus for small fractions.
- Tables as editable text, cited in order, captions with the tables, notes below, "Avoid vertical rules and shading within table cells", not duplicating results given elsewhere.
- Figures as separate files (Figure_1, Figure_2), cited in order, each with a caption (a brief title and a description; symbols explained); vector EPS or PDF, or bitmaps at 300 dpi (photographs) or 1,000 dpi (line drawings); not several figures combined in one file; colour accessible to readers with impaired colour vision.
- Fire Safety Journal only: SI units required ("If other units are mentioned ... provide the equivalent unit in SI"); its LaTeX template is encouraged; footnotes sparingly.
- Appendices lettered A, B, with equations, tables and figures numbered A.1, B.1.
- Language: American or British English, not a mix.

## 5. References

All three, the same text: any consistent style at submission; numbers in square brackets in the order of first citation; journal names abbreviated by the ISSN List of Title Word Abbreviations; DOIs where available; every reference must correspond to a real source; references cited in the abstract given in full; web references with the full URL and the date last accessed; datasets marked [dataset] with a persistent identifier; preprints marked as such. The journal's style is applied at proof.

## 6. Declarations

All three, the same text:
- Competing interests: the declarations tool "should always be completed", and the Word document it produces uploaded with the files; authors with none select "I have nothing to declare".
- Funding: listed in a standard form; with no funding, the sentence "This research did not receive any specific grant from funding agencies in the public, commercial, or not-for-profit sectors."
- Generative AI: its use "must be declared by adding a statement at the end of the manuscript when the paper is first submitted ... in a new section before the reference list", titled "Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process", with Elsevier's model statement; not required for basic grammar, spelling and reference tools; images made with generative AI disclosed in each caption as well; AI tools may create data visualisations "directly derived from underlying data using reproducible analytical, computational, or statistical methods".
- Author contributions by CRediT roles; acknowledgements in a separate section before the references.
- Research data: Option C, deposit in a repository and link it at submission, or state why not.
- Preprints: allowed and not prior publication. The Journal of Power Sources and the Journal of Energy Storage offer posting on SSRN during submission (the first "as soon as it passes the journal's initial desk review", the second "immediately"); the Fire Safety Journal page does not offer it.

## 7. Cover letter, institutional e-mail, pre-submission enquiries

- **Cover letter.** Only the Journal of Power Sources gives rules: "Keep it short (ideally less than one page) and focused"; state the aim and main findings; "Explain how your study fits the journal's aim and scope"; emphasise novelty and implications; it "should not include funding information, author declarations, or suggested or opposed reviewers". The other two guides say nothing about a cover letter.
- **Institutional e-mail.** None of the three guides states a rule requiring an institutional address; each requires the corresponding author's address on the title page, where it is published.
- **Pre-submission enquiries.** None of the three guides invites them; each links only Elsevier's general "Five pre-submission tips". None of the three editorial-board pages shows an e-mail address. The editors who lead each journal, by those pages:

## 8. The Journal of Power Sources' good practice guide for papers on batteries (the parts that bear on this paper)

- Introduction: state the background, aim and novelty; cite relevant and up-to-date literature ("results from references published more than five years ago ... cannot be considered up-to-date", three years for hot topics); no conclusion-style paragraph in the introduction.
- Models: "Research works reporting battery models need to give all details on the chosen form of mass, energy and momentum conservation", with control volumes "properly defined"; new constitutive equations and rate laws "examined in comparison with literature data or experiments"; "All relevant approximations used must be reported"; "all modeling/simulation results must be compared with experimental data, either originating from the same contribution or from existing contributions in the literature".
- General: emphatic words ("superior", "excellent", "outstanding") limited; figures consistent in colour and font.
