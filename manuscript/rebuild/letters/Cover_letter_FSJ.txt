Dear Professor Bisby and Professor Merci,

I submit the manuscript "The heat a neighbouring lithium-ion cell needs for thermal runaway propagation depends on how the cell is modelled" for consideration as a research article in the Fire Safety Journal.

Models of thermal runaway propagation in lithium-ion modules treat each cell either as one temperature or as a resolved temperature field, and where they include the heat of the failing cell's vented ejecta they choose what share of it reaches the neighbour. The manuscript shows, for one cell, one set of published inputs and one set of published tests, that the two treatments need different amounts of delivered heat before the neighbour follows, explains each with a reduced model, and names the measurement that would decide between them.

It models two touching LG M50 cells with published decomposition kinetics against sixteen published two-cell tests. With the failing cell's energy held to calorimetry, conduction and radiation leave the neighbour at least 29 K short of runaway, so the rest must come by other paths. In two tests the resolved neighbour needs 1.5 and 1.7 times the heat the lumped neighbour needs. Conduction into the surface layer sets the resolved neighbour's hot spot, while the lumped neighbour runs away once heated to 218.5 °C, where its reaction heat outruns its losses. The kinetics' published uncertainty moves that temperature by 6.1 K, while matching the two models needs every rate evaluated over 17.5 K hotter. Published inputs let every other path deliver 27.9 kJ or more, so neither the tests nor the inputs decide; a heat flux measured on the neighbour's facing side would. A reduced model of the resolved cell, a reactive cross-section, gave the same outcome as all 151 three-dimensional runs it was set against, at a fraction of their cost, which makes the resolved treatment affordable in a module model.

It falls within fire safety science and engineering as a quantitative account of the heat that carries thermal runaway from one cell to the next, and of the measurement that would settle it.

The code, inputs and run records are openly archived, and the study's 98 predictions were committed before the runs they concern.

I am a mechanical engineer (B.Tech 2026, COEP Technological University, Pune), working independently, with no funding and no competing interests. The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere.
