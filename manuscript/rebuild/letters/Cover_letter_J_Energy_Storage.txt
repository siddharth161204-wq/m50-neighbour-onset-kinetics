I submit the manuscript "Modelling thermal runaway propagation between lithium-ion cells: a lumped neighbour needs less heat than a resolved one after strong triggers" for consideration as a research article in the Journal of Energy Storage.

Propagation models of modules treat each cell as one temperature or a resolved field, and choose what share of the failing cell's vented heat reaches its neighbour. The manuscript asks how much delivered heat a neighbour needs under each treatment, for two touching LG M50 cells with decomposition kinetics fitted to calorimetry of NCM811 and of graphite with its electrolyte (the M50's negative electrode adds silicon), against sixteen published two-cell tests.

In this model the choice matters, in a direction set by the trigger. With each test's trigger fitted to its measured peak, the resolved neighbour's threshold is 1.5 and 1.7 times the lumped neighbour's in two tests with strong triggers (fitted body energies of 31.6 to 54.3 kJ), and 0.85 times in one with a weak trigger. A share of ejected energy calibrated with one treatment would therefore need a trigger-dependent correction to serve the other. Reduced models point to the mechanisms: conduction for the resolved cell's hot spot up to 120 s, and an energy balance to an ignition temperature of 218.5 to 230.4 °C for the lumped cell. In one test the resolved neighbour runs away with the lumped neighbour's energy only if every reaction runs as if over 17.5 K hotter; the kinetics' published uncertainty spans 6.1 K of ignition temperature and omits the difference between their materials and the M50's. At the central contact a two-dimensional reactive cross-section, set afterwards against all 151 archived three-dimensional runs, most far from a threshold, reproduced every outcome at a fraction of their cost; it puts the resolved threshold above the lumped in 13 of the 14 strong-trigger tests and below it after both weak triggers.

In the model, with the failing cell's energy held to calorimetry, conduction and radiation leave the neighbour at least 29 K short of runaway. Published inputs cap no route of heat to the neighbour's facing side below 27.9 kJ in the first 21.4 s, more than either treatment needs, so the sixteen tests cannot decide between them. The one published record of a propagating neighbour fits neither cleanly, its final rise starting 37 K cooler than on the resolved model's far side; a heat flux measured on its facing side, with the thermocouple's angle, could decide, if the model is otherwise right.

It falls within the journal's topics of modelling and of the safety of energy storage systems: it gives what a neighbouring cell in a module needs before it follows a failing one.

The code, inputs and run records are archived openly, with the study's 98 predictions, each committed before its runs.

I am a mechanical engineer (B.Tech 2026, COEP Technological University, Pune), working independently, with no funding and no competing interests. The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere.
