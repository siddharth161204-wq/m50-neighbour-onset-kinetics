I submit the manuscript "Modelling thermal runaway propagation between lithium-ion cells: a lumped neighbour needs less heat than a resolved one after strong triggers" for consideration as a research article in the Journal of Energy Storage.

Propagation models of modules treat each cell as one temperature or a resolved field, and choose what share of the failing cell's vented heat reaches its neighbour. The manuscript asks how much delivered heat a neighbour needs under each treatment, for two touching LG M50 cells with decomposition kinetics fitted to calorimetry of NCM811 and of graphite with its electrolyte (the M50's negative electrode adds silicon), against sixteen published two-cell tests. Earlier propagation models represent the cell one way or the other; the manuscript sets the two side by side on the same inputs and tests.

In this model the choice matters, in a direction set by the trigger. With each test's trigger fitted to its measured peak, the least delivered heat that runs the resolved neighbour away is at least 1.36 times the lumped neighbour's in two tests with strong triggers (fitted body energies of 31.6 to 54.3 kJ), and at most 0.91 times in one with a weak trigger. A share of ejected energy calibrated with one treatment would therefore need a trigger-dependent correction to serve the other. In one test the energy that runs the lumped neighbour away runs the resolved one away under none of the samples of the kinetics' uncertainty tried, and only if every reaction rate is taken over 17.5 K hotter. At the central contact a two-dimensional reduced model, set afterwards against all 151 earlier three-dimensional runs, most far from a threshold, reproduced every outcome at a fraction of their cost; it puts that heat higher for the resolved neighbour in 13 of the 14 strong-trigger tests and lower after both weak triggers.

In the model, with the failing cell's energy held to calorimetry, conduction and radiation leave the neighbour at least 29 K short of runaway. Published inputs cap no route of heat to the side of the neighbour facing the failing cell below 27.9 kJ in the first 21.4 s, more than either treatment needs, so the sixteen tests cannot decide between them. The one published record of a propagating neighbour fits neither cleanly, its final rise starting 37 K cooler than on the resolved model's far side; a heat flux measured where the neighbour faces the failing cell could decide, if the model is otherwise right.

It falls within the journal's topics of modelling and of the safety of energy storage systems: it gives what a neighbouring cell in a module needs before it follows a failing one.

The code, inputs and run records are archived openly, with the study's 98 predictions, each committed before its runs.

I am a mechanical engineer (B.Tech 2026, COEP Technological University, Pune), working independently, with no funding and no competing interests. The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere.
