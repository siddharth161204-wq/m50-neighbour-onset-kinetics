I submit the manuscript "Thermal runaway propagation between two lithium-ion cells: resolved and lumped neighbour models against sixteen tests" for consideration as a research article in the Journal of Energy Storage.

Propagation models must assume how much heat a failing cell gives its neighbour and whether each cell is one temperature (lumped) or a temperature field (resolved). The manuscript asks how much heat must reach a neighbour for it to follow, and whether published tests can say.

Sixteen published tests of two touching LG M50 cells are modelled with decomposition kinetics of NCM811 and graphite, each failing cell's energy fitted to its measured peak temperature: 31.6 to 54.3 kJ in fourteen tests (strong triggers) and 9.0 and 16.6 kJ in two (weak). With the neighbour either lumped or resolved in three dimensions, its need, the least heat that, delivered to its side facing the failing cell, runs it away by the test's measured time, is searched.

With the failing cell's energy fitted or held to 33.2 kJ, the most that the Battery Failure Databank's calorimetry gives for this cell, conduction and radiation run no resolved neighbour away. With heat delivered over 21.4 s, the mean rise time derived from two published records, the eight tests that propagated after strong triggers need 4.45 to 7.54 kJ if the neighbour is resolved, two of them its threshold, the least heat that runs it away at all, and 0.26 to 5.96 kJ if it is lumped. At nominal inputs, the resolved threshold is at least 1.36 times the lumped one after the four strong triggers searched in three dimensions and at most 0.91 times after both weak ones. Published inputs rule out no path delivering 27.9 kJ to the facing side within 21.4 s.

For readers who model propagation in modules, a neighbour's share of vented heat calibrated in a lumped model may therefore need correcting for a resolved neighbour, in a direction that goes with the failing cell's energy, and a heat flux measured on the neighbour's facing side could test the needs. The work falls within the journal's topics of modelling and of the safety of energy storage systems.

A separate manuscript of mine on a different question, the error of assuming uniform heat generation in the thermal model of a cell (EST-D-26-19130), is under consideration at the journal and shares no results or text with this one.
