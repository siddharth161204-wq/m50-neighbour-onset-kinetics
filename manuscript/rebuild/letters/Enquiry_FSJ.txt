Title: The heat a neighbouring lithium-ion cell needs for thermal runaway propagation depends on how the cell is modelled

Abstract: Propagation models treat a failing lithium-ion cell's neighbour as one temperature (lumped) or a resolved field. For two touching LG M50 cells with published kinetics, against sixteen two-cell tests, this paper asks how much heat each needs. With the failing cell's energy held to calorimetry, conduction and radiation leave the neighbour at least 29 K short; the rest must come from the ejecta or other paths. About 7 kJ over 21.4 s makes the resolved neighbour run away, 1.5 and 1.7 times the lumped one's need in two tests. Conduction into the surface layer sets the resolved neighbour's hot-spot threshold, within 3 % of a conduction model. The lumped neighbour runs away once heated to 218.5 °C, where reaction heat outruns losses. The kinetics' uncertainty moves that temperature by 6.1 K; matching the models needs every rate evaluated over 17.5 K hotter. At the measured times the resolved neighbour needs 4.1 to 8.1 kJ, the lumped one 0.2 to 6.0 kJ. Published inputs let every such path deliver 27.9 kJ or more, so neither tests nor inputs decide. The one published history jumps from 37 K below the resolved neighbour's far side. A facing-side heat flux measurement would decide.
