Title: Modelling thermal runaway propagation between lithium-ion cells: a lumped neighbour needs less heat than a resolved one after strong triggers

Abstract: Propagation models treat a lithium-ion cell as one temperature (lumped) or a resolved field, calibrating on tests the vented heat reaching a neighbour. Two touching LG M50 cells are modelled against sixteen tests, with kinetics fitted to NCM811 and graphite. With the failing cell's energy held to calorimetry, conduction and radiation leave the neighbour at least 29 K short. Fitted instead to each test's trigger peak, that energy gives resolved thresholds 1.5 and 1.7 times the lumped ones in two strong-trigger tests and 0.85 times after a weak trigger. In this model, heat calibrated with one neighbour needs a trigger-dependent correction for the other. In one test the resolved neighbour needs every reaction run as if over 17.5 K hotter to run away with the lumped one's energy; kinetic uncertainty spans 6.1 K of ignition temperature. At the central contact a two-dimensional reactive cross-section afterwards reproduced all 151 archived three-dimensional outcomes, most far from a threshold. The one published record of a propagating neighbour fits neither: its final rise starts 37 K cooler than on the resolved model's far side. The tests cannot decide; a facing-side heat flux and the thermocouple's angle could, if the other inputs stand.
