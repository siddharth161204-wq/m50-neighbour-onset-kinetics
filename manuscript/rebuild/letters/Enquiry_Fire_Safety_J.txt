Title: Modelling thermal runaway propagation between lithium-ion cells: a lumped neighbour needs less heat than a resolved one after strong triggers

Abstract: Propagation models treat a lithium-ion cell as one temperature (lumped) or a resolved field; two touching LG M50 cells are modelled against sixteen tests, with kinetics fitted to NCM811 and graphite. With the failing cell's energy held to calorimetry, conduction and radiation leave the neighbour at least 29 K short. Fitted to each test's trigger peak, that energy puts the resolved threshold above the lumped after strong triggers, 1.5 and 1.7 times in two three-dimensional searches and in 13 of 14 by a reduced model, and below it, 0.85 times, after weak ones. In this model, vented heat calibrated with one neighbour needs a trigger-dependent correction for the other. In one test the resolved neighbour needs every reaction run as if over 17.5 K hotter to run away with the lumped one's energy; kinetic uncertainty spans 6.1 K of ignition temperature. The reduced model, a two-dimensional reactive cross-section, afterwards reproduced all 151 archived three-dimensional outcomes. The one published record of a propagating neighbour fits neither: its final rise starts 37 K cooler than on the resolved model's far side. The tests cannot decide; a facing-side heat flux and the thermocouple's angle could, if the model is otherwise right.
