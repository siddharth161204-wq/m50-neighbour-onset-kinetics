Title: Thermal runaway propagation between lithium-ion cells: a lumped neighbour needs less heat than a resolved one after strong triggers

Abstract: Propagation models treat a lithium-ion cell as one temperature (lumped) or a resolved field. Modelling two touching LG M50 cells with kinetics of another chemistry, against sixteen tests, this paper finds the heat each neighbour needs. With the failing cell's energy held to calorimetry, conduction and radiation leave the neighbour at least 29 K short. With fitted triggers, the resolved neighbour needs about 7 kJ over 21.4 s in two strong-trigger tests, 1.5 and 1.7 times the lumped threshold, and 0.85 times after a weak trigger. Up to 120 s, surface conduction sets the hot-spot threshold, within 3 % of a conduction model. The lumped threshold follows an energy balance to 218.5 °C, where reaction heat outruns losses. Matching the thresholds in one test needs a cell's reaction rates over 17.5 K hotter, against 6.1 K of kinetic uncertainty. At the central contact a reactive cross-section reproduced all 151 three-dimensional outcomes. At the measured times, bar one test, it needs 4.1 to 8.1 kJ, the lumped neighbour 0.2 to 6.0 kJ, within published bounds. The one published record of a propagating neighbour jumps 37 K cooler than the resolved model's far side. A facing-side heat flux measurement would decide.
