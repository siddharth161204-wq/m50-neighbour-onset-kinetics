Title: Modelling thermal runaway propagation between lithium-ion cells: a lumped neighbour needs less heat than a resolved one after strong triggers

Abstract: Propagation models treat a lithium-ion cell as one temperature (lumped) or a resolved field; two touching LG M50 cells, with NCM811 and graphite kinetics, are modelled against sixteen tests. Held to calorimetry, the failing cell's (trigger's) energy leaves the neighbour at least 29 K short by conduction and radiation. Fitted instead to the trigger's measured peak temperature in each test, that energy, with central kinetics and properties, makes the least delivered heat for runaway larger for a resolved than a lumped neighbour after strong triggers, in 13 of 14 tests by a two-dimensional reduced model and at least 1.36 times in two three-dimensional searches, and smaller, at most 0.91 times, after weak ones. In this model, vented heat calibrated with one neighbour needs a trigger-dependent correction for the other. In one test the lumped neighbour's runaway energy runs the resolved one away under no kinetic sample tried, only with every reaction rate taken over 17.5 K hotter. The one published record of a propagating neighbour fits neither cleanly, its final rise starting 37 K cooler than the resolved model's. The tests cannot decide; a heat flux measured where the neighbour faces the trigger could, if the model is otherwise right.
