Title: Modelling thermal runaway propagation between lithium-ion cells: a lumped neighbour needs less heat than a resolved one after strong triggers

Abstract: Propagation models treat a lithium-ion cell as one temperature (lumped) or a resolved field, and calibrate on tests the vented heat reaching a neighbour. Modelling two touching LG M50 cells with kinetics fitted to another cell, against sixteen tests, this paper finds what each model needs. With the failing cell's energy held to calorimetry, conduction and radiation leave the neighbour at least 29 K short. With that energy fitted instead to each test's trigger peak, the resolved neighbour needs about 7 kJ in two strong-trigger tests, 1.5 and 1.7 times the lumped threshold, and 0.85 times after a weak trigger. Heat calibrated with one model thus needs a trigger-dependent correction for the other. Matching the two thresholds in one test needs every reaction to run as if over 17.5 K hotter, beyond the 6.1 K that the kinetics' uncertainty spans. At the central contact conductance a two-dimensional reactive cross-section reproduced all 151 three-dimensional outcomes. The one published record of a propagating neighbour fits neither model cleanly: its final rise starts 37 K cooler than on the resolved model's far side. The tests cannot decide between the models; a facing-side heat flux measurement would, if the other inputs stand.
