Title: Thermal runaway propagation between two lithium-ion cells: resolved and lumped neighbour models against sixteen tests

Abstract: How much heat must a failing cell's neighbour receive to follow it? Sixteen tests of two touching LG M50 cells are modelled with NCM811/graphite kinetics, the neighbour lumped (one temperature) or resolved (three-dimensional), each failing cell's energy fitted to its own measured peak temperature: 31.6 to 54.3 kJ (strong triggers, fourteen tests) or 9.0 and 16.6 kJ (weak). By conduction and radiation, a failing cell releasing at most 33.2 kJ, calorimetry's largest for this cell, leaves the neighbour at least 29 K short of runaway. Over 21.4 s, the mean of the two rise times read from the two published records, 4.45 to 7.54 kJ through its facing side runs it away when the eight propagating strong-trigger tests did if resolved and 0.26 to 5.96 kJ if lumped: 5.8 to 9.8 % and 0.3 to 7.8 % of the Databank's median M50 ejecta energy, 76.74 kJ. To run away at all, the resolved neighbour needs at least 1.36 times the lumped one's energy after four strong triggers searched in three dimensions, at most 0.91 times after both weak ones. Published inputs do not exclude any path delivering 27.9 kJ there within 21.4 s; a heat flux measured there could test the needs.
