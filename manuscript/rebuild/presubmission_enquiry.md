Title: Thermal runaway propagation between two lithium-ion cells: resolved and lumped neighbour models against sixteen tests

Abstract: How much of a failing cell's vented heat must reach its neighbour for it to follow, and can published tests say? Two touching LG M50 cells are modelled with published kinetics against sixteen two-cell tests, the neighbour lumped (one temperature) or resolved (a temperature field). Held to calorimetry, the failing cell's (trigger's) energy leaves the neighbour at least 29 K short of runaway by conduction and radiation. With triggers fitted to measured peaks, strong-trigger tests need 4.1 to 8.1 kJ by the measured propagation time if the neighbour is resolved, by a reduced model, and 0.2 to 6.0 kJ if lumped, 5.3 to 10.6 % and 0.3 to 7.8 % of the ejecta's measured median, 76.74 kJ. With central kinetics and properties and a 21.4 s pulse, the resolved runaway threshold is 1.36 to 2.27 times the lumped one in two strong-trigger tests and 0.80 to 0.91 times in a weak-trigger one, so a share calibrated with one needs a trigger-dependent correction for the other. Published inputs bound no path to the neighbour's facing half below 27.9 kJ within 21.4 s. A heat flux measured there, to well within the readings' difference, would remove the dependence if the model is otherwise right.
