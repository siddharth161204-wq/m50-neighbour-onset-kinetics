Title: Thermal runaway propagation between lithium-ion cells: a lumped neighbour needs less heat than a resolved one after strong triggers

Abstract: Propagation models treat a lithium-ion cell as one temperature (lumped) or a resolved field, and calibrate on tests the vented heat reaching a neighbour. Modelling two touching LG M50 cells with another chemistry's kinetics against sixteen tests, this paper finds the heat each model needs. With the failing cell's energy held to calorimetry, conduction and radiation leave the neighbour at least 29 K short. With that energy fitted instead to each test's trigger peak, the resolved neighbour needs about 7 kJ over 21.4 s in two strong-trigger tests, 1.5 and 1.7 times the lumped threshold, and 0.85 times after a weak trigger. Heat calibrated with one model thus cannot serve the other without a trigger-dependent correction. The lumped cell ignites at 218.5 °C, where reaction heat outruns losses. Matching the two thresholds in one test needs rates over 17.5 K hotter, beyond the kinetics' 6.1 K span. At the central contact conductance a two-dimensional reactive cross-section reproduced all 151 three-dimensional outcomes. The one published record of a propagating neighbour starts its final rise 37 K cooler than the resolved model's far side does. The tests cannot decide between the models; a facing-side heat flux measurement would.
