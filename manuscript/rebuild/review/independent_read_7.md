Brief 30, item 10, second round. A separate agent, new and with no sight of the work, of reads 1 to 6 or of the editor's reads, read only the built manuscript (34 pages) and supplement (40 pages) as PDFs, as built at commit f4d8ee6 (Brief 30's second round), with no other file, command or web access, and listed untraceable numbers (A), archive-dependent sentences (B), overclaims (C) and other first-reading objections (D), under the instructions of reads 2 to 6, changed only in the page counts. Coverage: I had already read both files in full before the interruption. That was the manuscript in ranges 1–17 and 18–34, and the supplement in ranges 1–20 and 21–40, so nothing needed re-reading. I opened no other file. MS = manuscript, SM = supplement.

Verdict: Not ready. No number is strictly untraceable. About seventeen numbers can't be checked because they appear in no table or figure (listed as borderline under A). The main text uses step numbers in three places, and several supplement passages rely on the archive. Four sentences overclaim outright (C1–C4) and seven more are borderline. The biggest first-reading problems:
- Only 1 of the 16 fitted triggers lies inside the measured body-energy range.
- The AI-use declaration is empty.
- The measured jump onset is given two different ways.

Strictly untraceable: none. Every input has a citation or an "assumed" mark. Every result is tabulated, plotted, or stated as the output of a described run or computation.

C. OVERCLAIMS

C1. MS p.1: "With a 21.4 s pulse (the triggers' measured rise time)". The 21.39 s figure was not measured. It is the mean of two rise times the author derived from a figure (temperature rise over a printed mean heating rate) for only 2 of the 16 tests (SM p.19: 17.53 and 25.24 s). It is then applied to all sixteen tests. Table 2 (MS p.8) also cites [16] for it.
C2. MS p.1, title: "resolved and lumped neighbour models against sixteen tests". Yet MS p.27 says "Much of what is compared is model against model". Only one history is compared with a measured temperature, and the held-out test shows no skill (MS p.13). Borderline.
C3. MS p.25: "One measurement could decide between the readings". The same claim appears in the abstract (p.1: "A heat flux measured there ... could decide between them"), on p.28, and in heading 4.3 ("The measurements that would decide"). The problems:
- The needs are model quantities. They depend on chosen delivery regions and shapes, on triggers fitted beyond calorimetry, and on a reduced model that is up to 34.5 s off in time.
- A gauge would also record the trigger body's own radiation, which the model counts separately.
- Nothing shows that a measured flux maps onto a need. The hedge "if the model holds otherwise" is doing all the work.
C4. MS p.26: "The flame's position and temperature would bound radiation by geometry, from 27.9 kJ toward 0.85 kJ". This contradicts SM p.17, which says "With the holder able to reflect, the geometry stops mattering". SM p.17 also says the share near the facing edge "rises toward a quarter".
C5. MS p.25: "in a direction that in the four tests searched in three dimensions went with the trigger's strength". Also "a correction whose sign went with the trigger". The reversal rests on one 3D test, ST-Exp3. Its fitted 16.6 kJ is below the Databank's smallest measured 18.67 kJ (SM p.8). Its trigger peaked at only 282 °C, yet its neighbour propagated (Table 1). Borderline.
C6. MS p.1: "Published inputs cannot cap any path to that half below 27.9 kJ within 21.4 s". Also MS p.27: "cannot bound at least four delivery paths below 27.9 kJ". Both are stated as firm floors, but SM p.17 calls the bounds "estimates of that most rather than strict limits". Borderline.
C7. MS p.17: "The lumped threshold is broadly consistent with an energy balance to an ignition temperature". The support is weaker than it reads:
- Under the max(10 %, 300 J) tolerance, 9 of the 32 pass because both are zero.
- PT-Exp2 at the high contact passes at about 3× (0.29 vs 0.10 kJ, SM p.25).
- The low-contact offset tracks trigger strength.
- The registered slope of 150–300 J per kJ failed.
Borderline.
C8. MS p.25: "Under either reading one delivered share cannot meet every test's need at its measured time". This is inferred from the spread of needs; no run applied a single share across all tests. Borderline.
C9. MS p.24: "Either way the needed energies are at most about a quarter of what the ejecta carry". This is relative to the Databank median and depends on the fitted triggers. Against ST-Exp3's own ejected energy (51.5 kJ), the needs are 24–36 %. Borderline.
C10. MS p.17: "with it goes a difference in what each neighbour takes in". This general association is drawn from three runs without delivery. Borderline.
C11. MS p.27 (Conclusion 5): "gave the outcome of the three-dimensional model in all 151 archived runs it was set against afterwards". The comparison "was made after those runs" (MS p.21), and 82 of the 92 runaway cases are far above threshold. Yet this model then supplies every resolved need (MS p.26). The labelling is honest; borderline.

D. OTHER FIRST-READING OBJECTIONS

D1. MS p.30: "Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process". The heading has no statement under it.
D2. MS p.29: "Conceptualization, Methodology, Investigation, Resources, Validation, Project administration, Writing – review & editing". For a sole author it lacks Writing – original draft, Software and Formal analysis.
D3. MS p.4: "In thirteen of the sixteen tests it exceeds 33.2 kJ". SM p.8 gives the Databank's smallest measured body energy as 18.67 kJ. So only PT-Exp3 (31.6 kJ) lies inside the measured range: thirteen triggers are above it and two are below it (9.0 and 16.6 kJ). MS p.26 never mentions the two below. The abstract never says its needs and ratios rest on these triggers.
D4. MS p.1: "the 76.74 kJ measured in M50 ejecta". This is the median of ten tests. The abstract also omits that the kinetics come from a different chemistry than the M50's (MS p.26).
D5. MS p.1: "at least 29 K short of runaway (255.7 °C)". This is the hottest point's margin, yet MS p.5 says "a hottest point that reaches it need not run the cell away". Also, minor: SM p.9's 226.75 °C gives 28.95 K.
D6. MS p.8: "the Databank's cells weighed 68.05 to 68.52 g before their tests", yet 65 g is assumed. No sensitivity is shown for the sector, emissivity, convection or can properties.
D7. MS p.9: "reaches the source's internal-short rate, 1 K min−1 of self-heating, at 199.8 °C". Yet 255.7 °C is the runaway level. No sensitivity is shown to the level or to the 900 s window.
D8. MS p.18: "197.3 °C at 271.5 s, when the neighbour's jump begins". The paper's own rule (SM p.19) gives "267.5 s and 186.54 °C". The text then quotes both 36.8 K and 47.6 K.
D9. MS p.21: "at most 20.7 K lower". MS p.20 says "20.9 K below" for the same run, because one comparison stops at 332 s and the other at 900 s. MS p.21 also omits that the cross-section's peak is up to 3.8 K higher in some runs (Table S14, PT-Exp1 360 s 9.80 kJ: 140.6 vs 144.4 °C).
D10. MS p.11: "four of them known beforehand to be fixed, or nearly". There are other near-certain holds. Step 15 P1 "holds partly by construction" (SM p.9). For step 14 P6, the analysis gives 65,520 J against a 1 kJ test. Also, "foreseen in the files' own analyses" is wrong for three of the six (step 8 P3, step 9 P4 and P5), whose foreseeing is in the expectation; SM p.7 distinguishes the two.
D11. SM p.8: "The other 46 are those of steps 1 to 4 and 6 and those of steps 7 to 11...". The unlisted predictions failed 24/46, against 18/52 for the listed ones. Step 8's discharge runs are unreported. And MS p.11 says the triggers, delivered heat and mean criterion "entered ... after earlier predictions failed", so the final analysis was not pre-registered as a whole.
D12. SM p.4: "E* in step 12, a different quantity from the threshold above". One label is used for two quantities.
D13. SM p.7: "judged about the emulation's onset under the posterior mean, 179.3 °C, as the band is about its mean". The amended check's pass rule cannot be reconstructed from this.
D14. MS p.29: "their grid was not refined in a separate check". The cross-section models give 12 of the 16 resolved thresholds. For the 3D model (SM p.16), mesh halving was done on one run per step, and only its effect on the peak rise was reported, not on thresholds or needs. Step 15 used only a 1.5× refinement.
D15. MS p.14 (Fig. 2 caption): "5.5 s earlier to 34.5 s later ... more than the 10 s of a match". The headline resolved needs come from this reduced model. Only one 3D run checks an energy at need level.
D16. SM p.16: "a value measured on another cell widens a range and sets no value for this one". Yet the 225.8 kJ bound adds another cell's 136.5 kJ, and the 1500 °C and 398.5 m s−1 values also come from other cells or a module. MS p.10 calls each bound "the most that the published inputs surveyed allow", while SM p.17 calls them estimates.
D17. MS p.4: "the same tests are therefore also run with the body energy held to 33.2 kJ". But MS p.26 says "only the runs of Section 3.1 were made".
D18. MS p.23 (Fig. 6): "the lumped neighbour's needs at the measured times with the pulse". The resolved band covers four shapes, so the two bands are not comparable. The needs are also set against a 21.4 s bound.
D19. Figures:
- Fig. 2a (MS p.14): "a dot inside the marker; t in Supplementary Table S7". The t and b marks are unreadable at print size.
- Fig. 3 needs its caption to decode the markers.
- Fig. 4b does not say which values its "J less per kJ" comes from.
- Fig. 7a's legend sits in panel b.
- Fig. 1b's legend overlaps the lumped curve.
D20. SM p.25: "its definition depends on the outcome it is set to predict". This held-out predictor is circular, and an 8-vs-8 split has little power.
D21. SM p.19: "a spike to 102.0 °C at 3.1 s as the trigger runs away". The record's zero time is not aligned with the model's (MS p.3: "times counted from the trigger's runaway"). The record also starts at 32.2 °C against the model's 25 °C, and the RMS absorbs this offset.
D22. SM p.20: "One other dataset has a cell they cover: Read, Jones and Marco". It is not used even as a check, though PT-Exp4, also a sidewall-rupture test, is.
D23. MS p.3: "the mean of the trigger's rise times in the two records". The two records are 44 % apart, yet one 21.39 s release is used for all sixteen tests, with no threshold sensitivity to it. The aluminium holder (SM p.18) is not modelled as a heat sink.
D24. MS p.4, Eq. (1): "Gc the conductance of the contact and its air wedge integrated over the can". No number is given.
D25. Wording and selection:
- MS p.9: "Over the 164 of the 200 draws below whose emulated test reaches" is garbled.
- Table 2: "0.15 of the source's normalised amount (fresh)" is ambiguous.
- MS p.21 and SM p.28: "carried past the hottest point's crossing" does not fit runs that never cross (e.g. PT-Exp1 at 0–4.18 kJ, Table S14). Why the 151 runs come only from the step 9–11 searches is not explained.
D26. MS p.15: "Its resolved threshold lies between 6.71 and 7.42 kJ in PT-Exp4 and between...". Many Results sentences carry five to ten numbers inside nested qualifiers, which a reviewer will find hard to follow.
