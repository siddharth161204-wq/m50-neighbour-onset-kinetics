List 3. Borderline items

3.1 Manuscript p. 1 (abstract): "conduction and radiation run no resolved neighbour away at either energy". Also p. 12 (Section 3.1): "…and for the resolved reading under the posterior mean with the fitted triggers". At the high contact, ten of the sixteen no-delivery outcomes, including eight of the nine tests that propagated, rest on the reactive cross-section alone. That model was checked against 3D only at the central contact, and the fitted-trigger runs used only the posterior mean (Supp. S16 p. 38, Table S15 p. 39). The claim is therefore likely but not shown in 3D: the cross-section's highest mean there, 199.2 °C, is 56 K short. Section 3.1 states this limit; the abstract does not.

3.2 Manuscript p. 1 (abstract): "so here a heat share calibrated with either needs a trigger-dependent correction for the other". Also p. 26: "a correction whose sign went with the trigger". In 3D the reversal rests on the two weak-trigger tests, both fitted below the calorimetry range, at one parameter set. The resolved threshold was searched under no draw, while Table S12 (p. 33) shows the lumped threshold alone moving about fivefold across draws (PP-Exp3, 0.85 to 4.26 kJ). So "needs" goes slightly past what one parameter set can show, even with "here" and "at nominal inputs".

3.3 Manuscript p. 26 (Section 4.2) and p. 29 (Conclusion 5): the cross-section gives "the needs with the pulse to within 10% of the three-dimensional brackets' midpoints". The cross-section's need is the upper end of its own bracket, which is 0.62 to 0.84 kJ wide (p. 24; Table 3 caption, p. 15), or 8 to 18% of the needs. The −1.5 to +8.6% agreement holds for that upper end but is finer than the reduced model's own resolution. For example, SP-Exp3 has an upper end of 4.58 kJ, a possible lower end near 3.74 kJ, and a 3D midpoint of 4.515 kJ.

3.4 Manuscript p. 26 (Section 4.1): "Either way the needed energies are at most about a quarter of what the ejecta carry". This is true against the Databank median of 76.74 kJ that the sentence uses. Against the per-test ejected energy the model itself defines (3.105 × E_b, p. 4), it fails for ST-Exp3: the cross-section delivery over 240 s, 18.77 kJ in all (Table S7, p. 24), is about 36% of its 51.5 kJ.

3.5 Manuscript p. 20 (Section 3.6) and Table 4 (p. 22), on holder conduction: "energy conservation alone capping it at 247.3 kJ, all that the trigger can release". 247.3 kJ is the Databank's largest total (110.8 kJ) plus another cell's heat of burning (136.5 kJ). The paper's own fitted triggers imply body plus ejected energy up to 54.3 + 168.6 = 222.9 kJ before burning, so by its own numbers this is not a cap on these triggers. The Table 4 caption concedes the point only for the bounds set by the ejecta.

3.6 Manuscript p. 28 (Conclusion 1): "at least 28.9 K short of 255.7 °C". Section 3.1 (p. 12) and Table S17 (p. 44) give 226.7 °C and 29.0 K. 28.9 K can be reached only from the step-6 peak of 226.75 °C quoted in Supp. S3 (p. 9), and the main text does not report those runs.

3.7 Manuscript p. 18 (Section 3.4) with Fig. 4b (p. 19): "the threshold falls by 393 to 456 J for each kJ added (PT-Exp3, ST-Exp2, PP-Exp3)". The slopes are printed on Fig. 4b. The thresholds at the ±2 kJ shifts are plotted without values or brackets and appear in no table (Table S17 leaves them out), so neither the three-figure slopes nor their precision can be checked.

3.8 Supplement pp. 44–46 (Table S17) is the only trace for many main-text results. Examples: p. 12's 226.7 °C, 180.6 °C, 177.4 °C, 21.7 s and 162.6 °C; p. 16's 61.5 s; p. 18's 316.5 s, 240.1 °C and 2.1 to 2.3 kJ; p. 20's 241.4 °C and 16.3, 16.8 and 51.4 K.
