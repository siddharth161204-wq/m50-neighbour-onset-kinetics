The paper models sixteen published tests in which one of two touching LG M50 cells is driven into thermal runaway, using NCM811/graphite decomposition kinetics, to ask how much heat must reach the neighbour's facing side for it to follow by the measured time, how that answer changes when the neighbour is treated as one temperature (lumped) rather than as a three-dimensional temperature field (resolved), and whether the published records can say which heat path supplies that heat. It finds that conduction and radiation alone never run the resolved neighbour away, whether the failing cell's energy is fitted to its peak temperature, which puts most fits above the 33.2 kJ calorimetric maximum, or capped at that value; that with heat delivered over 21.4 s the eight propagating strong-trigger tests need 4.45 to 7.54 kJ with a resolved neighbour but 0.26 to 5.96 kJ with a lumped one; that the resolved threshold is at least 1.36 times the lumped one after four strong triggers but at most 0.91 times it after the two weak ones, so a vent-heat share calibrated in a lumped model may need a trigger-dependent correction in a resolved one; and that published inputs rule out no path delivering 27.9 kJ to the facing side within 21.4 s, leaving a facing-side heat-flux measurement as the proposed test. I would send it to review, though not by a wide margin, and what decides it is that the paper sits squarely within the journal's modelling and safety topics and puts specific, checkable numbers, anchored to sixteen tests, on an assumption that module-level propagation models routinely make, whereas the things that pull towards declining, namely fitted trigger energies that mostly exceed the calorimetric maximum with only a tentative explanation, a lumped-versus-resolved direction resting on four strong and two weak tests, a closing result that the data cannot tell heat paths apart, and an abstract whose coined, compressed phrasing makes the reader work for the result, are weaknesses for reviewers to test rather than reasons to decline it without review.
