The paper is a computational study of thermal runaway propagation between two touching LG M50 cells, which models sixteen published two-cell tests with NCM811/graphite kinetics fitted to calorimetry, fits each failing cell's energy to its own measured peak temperature, and solves the neighbour either as one lumped temperature or as a resolved three-dimensional field, in order to find how much heat must reach the neighbour through its facing side for it to follow the failing cell into runaway. It finds that conduction and radiation from a failing cell releasing 33.2 kJ, the largest calorimetric value for this cell, leave the neighbour at least 29 K short of runaway, that reproducing the eight strong-trigger tests that propagated instead takes 4.45 to 7.54 kJ through the facing side over 21.4 s if the neighbour is resolved and 0.26 to 5.96 kJ if it is lumped, all under a tenth of the median M50 ejecta energy of 76.74 kJ, that the resolved neighbour's runaway threshold is at least 1.36 times the lumped one's after the four strong triggers searched in three dimensions but at most 0.91 times after the two weak ones, and that published inputs exclude no path from delivering as much as 27.9 kJ to that side within 21.4 s, so it proposes a heat-flux measurement there as the test. I would decline it without review: the question sits within the journal's safety scope and the openly archived, pre-registered comparison with sixteen tests is a real strength, but what decides it is that the experiments calibrate the model rather than check it, with trigger energies fitted to peak temperatures and the neighbour's needs searched to match measured runaway times, that its headline 29 K shortfall rests on a 33.2 kJ calorimetric maximum while its own fits put strong triggers at up to 54.3 kJ, a gap neither the abstract nor the letter explains, and that it ends by stating that published inputs cannot tell the candidate heat paths apart, so it reads as a careful but inconclusive re-analysis for a single cell type whose dense, number-led abstract leaves the advance for pack modellers hard to see.
