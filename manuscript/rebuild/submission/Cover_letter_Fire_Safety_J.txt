Dear Professor Bisby and Professor Merci,

I submit the manuscript "Thermal runaway propagation between two lithium-ion cells: resolved and lumped neighbour models against sixteen tests" for consideration as a research article in the Fire Safety Journal.

Propagation models of modules choose what share of a failing cell's vented heat reaches its neighbour, a share the published tests do not measure, and treat each cell as one temperature or as a resolved field. The manuscript asks how much of that heat must reach a neighbour for it to follow, and whether published tests can say, for two touching LG M50 cells, with kinetics fitted to calorimetry of NCM811 and of graphite with electrolyte (the M50's negative electrode adds silicon), against sixteen published two-cell tests.

In the model, with the failing cell's energy held to calorimetry, conduction and radiation leave the neighbour at least 29 K short of runaway. With each trigger fitted to its test's measured peak, the model's neighbour needs, to run away when the tests propagated after strong triggers (31.6 to 54.3 kJ), 4.1 to 8.1 kJ through its side facing the failing cell if resolved, by a two-dimensional reduced model, and 0.2 to 6.0 kJ if lumped: 5.3 to 10.6 % and 0.3 to 7.8 % of the median 76.74 kJ that calorimetry measured in this cell's ejecta.

That estimate depends on the neighbour's treatment. With a 21.4 s pulse the least delivered heat that runs the resolved neighbour away is 1.36 to 2.27 times the lumped neighbour's in two strong-trigger tests searched in three dimensions and 0.80 to 0.91 times in one with a weak trigger, so in these tests a share calibrated with one treatment would need a correction, whose sign went with the trigger, to serve the other. Propagation models treat the cell one way or the other; here the two share every input. The reduced model reproduced the outcome of all 151 earlier three-dimensional runs, most of them far from a threshold, at a fraction of their cost.

Published inputs cap no path of the ejecta's heat to the facing side below 27.9 kJ within 21.4 s, more than either treatment needs, so the sixteen tests cannot remove that dependence; a heat flux measured where the neighbour faces the failing cell, to well within the 1.7 to 4.3 kJ by which the two treatments' needs differ, could, if the model is otherwise right and the resolved needs are computed in three dimensions.

It falls within fire safety science and engineering as a quantitative account of the heat that carries thermal runaway from one cell to the next.

The code, inputs and run records are archived openly, with the study's 98 predictions, each committed before its runs.

I am a mechanical engineer (B.Tech 2026, COEP Technological University, Pune), working independently, with no funding and no competing interests. The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere.
