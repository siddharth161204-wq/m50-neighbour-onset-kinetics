Title: Thermal runaway propagation between two lithium-ion cells: resolved and lumped neighbour models against sixteen tests

Abstract: Propagation models must assume the heat a failing cell's neighbour receives, unmeasured in published tests, and whether each cell is one temperature. Sixteen tests of touching LG M50 pairs are modelled with NCM811/graphite kinetics, the neighbour lumped or resolved in three dimensions. Fitted to peak temperatures, failing-cell energies are 31.6 to 54.3 kJ in fourteen (strong triggers), mostly above calorimetry's 33.2 kJ maximum, and 9.0 and 16.6 kJ in two (weak); conduction and radiation run no resolved neighbour away at either energy. Over 21.4 s, the two published records' mean rise time, 4.45 to 7.54 kJ through the facing side runs the resolved neighbour away by the eight propagating strong-trigger tests' measured times (two at its threshold, the least that runs it away at all), and 0.26 to 5.96 kJ the lumped one. The resolved threshold is at least 1.36 times the lumped one after four strong triggers searched in three dimensions and at most 0.91 times after both weak ones, at nominal inputs, so here a heat share calibrated with either may need a trigger-dependent correction for the other. Published inputs rule out no path delivering 27.9 kJ within 21.4 s; a facing-side heat flux could test the needs.
