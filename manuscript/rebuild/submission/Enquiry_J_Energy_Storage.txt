Title: Thermal runaway propagation between two lithium-ion cells: resolved and lumped neighbour models against sixteen tests

Abstract: How much vented heat must a failing cell's neighbour receive to follow, and can published tests say? Two touching LG M50 cells are modelled with published kinetics against sixteen two-cell tests, each failing cell (trigger) fitted to its test's peak, the neighbour lumped (one temperature) or resolved (a temperature field). Held to calorimetry, the trigger's conduction and radiation leave the modelled neighbour at least 29 K short of runaway (255.7 °C). To run away at the measured times after strong triggers (31.6 to 54.3 kJ), it needs 4.1 to 8.1 kJ through its facing half if resolved (by a reduced model) and 0.2 to 6.0 kJ if lumped, 5.3 to 10.6 % and 0.3 to 7.8 % of the 76.74 kJ measured in M50 ejecta. With a 21.4 s pulse (the triggers' measured rise time), its least runaway energy is 1.36 to 2.27 times as high resolved as lumped in two strong-trigger tests but 0.80 to 0.91 times in a weak-trigger test. Published inputs cannot cap any path to that half below 27.9 kJ within 21.4 s, above both needs. A heat flux measured there, resolving the 1.7 to 4.3 kJ between the needs, could decide between them, if the model holds otherwise.
