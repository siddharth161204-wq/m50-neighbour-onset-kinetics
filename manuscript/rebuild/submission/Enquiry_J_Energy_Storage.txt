Title: Thermal runaway propagation between two lithium-ion cells: resolved and lumped neighbour models against sixteen tests

Abstract: Propagation models must assume the heat a failing cell's neighbour receives, unmeasured in published tests, and whether each cell is one temperature. Sixteen tests of touching LG M50 pairs are modelled both ways, lumped or resolved in three dimensions, with NCM811/graphite kinetics. Fitted to peak temperatures, failing-cell energies are 31.6 to 54.3 kJ in fourteen (strong triggers), mostly above calorimetry's 33.2 kJ maximum, and 9.0 and 16.6 kJ in two (weak); conduction and radiation run no resolved neighbour away at either energy. Over 21.4 s, the mean of the two rise times read from the two published records, 4.45 to 7.54 kJ through the facing side runs the resolved neighbour away when the eight propagating strong-trigger tests did, and 0.26 to 5.96 kJ the lumped one. To run away at all, the resolved neighbour needs at least 1.36 times the lumped one's energy after four strong triggers, at most 0.91 times after both weak ones, searched in three dimensions, so here a heat share calibrated with one representation needs a trigger-dependent correction for the other. Published inputs rule out no path delivering 27.9 kJ within 21.4 s; a heat flux measured on the facing side could test the needs.
