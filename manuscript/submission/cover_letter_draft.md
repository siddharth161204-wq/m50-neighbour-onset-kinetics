Dear Editor,

I submit for consideration in the Journal of The Electrochemical Society the manuscript "Neither conduction nor a fixed delivered fraction reproduces sixteen two-cell propagation tests: a pre-registered study".

It reports a pre-registered modelling study in which the published Bayesian decomposition kinetics of Koenig, Zhao and Deng (Chem. Eng. J., 2025) are integrated in a resolved and a lumped neighbour LG M50 cell and set, test by test, against the sixteen two-cell propagation tests of Fransson et al. (J. Power Sources, 2024). Conduction and radiation alone do not ignite the neighbour at the energies calorimetry gives for this cell, also when each test's trigger is held to the largest body energy the Battery Failure Databank measured. A delivered part of the trigger's ejected energy is needed, and the part each test needs varies 42- to 208-fold between tests, or 22- to 64-fold in energy, overlapping between the tests that propagated and those that did not; in the four tests drawn, the kinetic posterior and the published thermal ranges move it by less than a third of its difference between tests. A local criterion gets the most outcomes right but almost none of the times, and a whole-cell criterion can match the times near its threshold but predicts few propagations; held out, nothing the model yields predicts the outcomes better than one probability for every test. The paper states what a propagation test would need to report for such a comparison to be decisive, and what the results imply for pack models.

Every stage's predictions were committed to a public repository before its runs; of 74, 36 failed, and all are reported. The code, inputs, run records and checks are archived on Zenodo under the DOI given in the data availability statement. The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. Generative AI was used in the research and in drafting the text, as disclosed in the Methods and the Acknowledgments; I revised the text and take full responsibility for its content.
