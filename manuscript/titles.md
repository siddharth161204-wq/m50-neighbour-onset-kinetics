# Candidate titles

Titles of at most 15 words for the Journal of The Electrochemical Society, hyphenated words counted as one (Brief 21, M1). The first is in the manuscript; the author chooses.

1. Neither conduction nor a fixed delivered fraction reproduces sixteen two-cell propagation tests: a pre-registered study (15 words)
2. Local and whole-cell ignition criteria tested against sixteen two-cell LG M50 propagation tests (13 words)
3. How much ejected energy must reach a neighbour cell? Sixteen two-cell propagation tests, read inversely (15 words)

Why the first is preferred: it states the two negative results the paper rests on, both of which hold in the files and both of which Stage 12 confirmed with the trigger's energies held to the calorimetry (no run with the calorimetry's largest body energy ignites a neighbour, in any test, at the central or the high contact; the propagated and non-propagated tests overlap in the fraction they need, as a share of each test's fitted ejected energy and of one measured ejected energy), and it names the pre-registration, which is what distinguishes the study. The second is the most neutral. The third leads with the inverse reading, the paper's most reusable number.

"Conduction" in the first title means conduction and radiation from the trigger's body; with each test's own, larger trigger, conduction alone does ignite the hot spot in eight tests, minutes early, so the title's claim is that conduction does not reproduce the tests, not that it never ignites.
