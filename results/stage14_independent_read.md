## Verdict

Every registered number in D158 to D161 and in the bounds, tests, module table and scorecard files follows from `results/stage14_inputs.csv` and the relations of D153 as written: separate code reproduces each of them (the ray-traced shares within sampling error, everything else exactly), the study's own scripts reproduce their committed outputs byte for byte, the needs are what the earlier result files hold, and all 372 B1 verdicts, B2's statements of fact and the six scores (two failed, four held; both failures recorded beforehand as fixed by the design) come out the same. As computations of the registered design, every registered number and verdict stands.

Three things do not stand as written. First, not every registered upper bound is what the published inputs cannot be made to exceed: A1 leaves out reflected radiation and the second venting end, A2 leaves out the radiation of the wedge gas along the rays to the trigger and applies a whole-cylinder mean to one half, and A6 leaves out two terms. Taken at their upper ends under the stage's own rules, these raise A1 within 21.39 s from 8,979.1 J to as much as 27,856 J and A2 within 21.39 s from 101,778.8 J to 111,686 J or more, and they change four B1 verdicts for A1 (one of them, M50_ST_Exp3 within 21.39 s, through the second venting end alone), but none of P1 to P6. Second, P5's verdict depends on the second sentence of D153 (3); its first sentence, applied to A5 as to A3 and A4, would make every path determinable and P5 fail. Third, the readings beside P1 for a flame held in front of the trigger's end face (27,490.0 J) and on the vent's axis (821.0 J) are maxima of the grid, not maxima: the first reaches at least 51.3 kJ and tends to 56.5 kJ, the second about 849 J.
